Choose how many doubles and triples you want to mark and see the number of lines and the cost.

How it works

Each double multiplies the lines by 2 and each triple by 3: lines = 3triples × 2doubles. The cost is the number of lines multiplied by the price per line.

For example, 2 triples and 3 doubles make 3 × 3 × 2 × 2 × 2 = 72 lines. At 1.00 per line, that costs 72.00.

Where the probabilities come from

If a match has odds, Quiniela Lab removes the bookmaker’s margin and uses that implied probability to estimate each sign (1X2). You can change the odds weight in the settings.

If you also enter the teams’ goals, you’ll see a Dixon-Coles model as a reference. It’s a variant of the Poisson distribution that corrects low scores: with a negative ρ, 0-0 and 1-1 become more likely, and so does X. It takes into account each team’s attack and defence, the home factor and, if you enter it, the value of the starting XIs.

What it can and can’t tell you

These figures are estimates, not certainties. They help you decide where to spend your doubles and triples within your budget, for example on the closest matches. No match is a sure thing, and a higher chance of getting matches right doesn’t mean a higher expected prize.

In our test of 90 European matches, bookmakers’ favourites won 62% of the time and the model’s 53%. That’s why, by default, odds take priority when they’re available.

Place them where they matter

The table shows what a combination costs, not where to put it. Quiniela Lab lets you set a budget and see an allocation based on odds-implied probabilities. A higher chance of getting matches right doesn’t mean a higher expected prize.
